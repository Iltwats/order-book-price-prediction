# TRAIN and masked validation: what the evidence supports

Verified 2026-09-13. This is a bounded research memo, not a new experiment declaration. No dataset rows were read, models fitted, or protocol settings changed.

**The scoring mask's use is documented; its generation is not explained in the three current official pages reviewed. TRAIN alone cannot identify an unknown selection rule.** Better all-row TRAIN correlation consequently does not establish better masked validation WP.

The live [Data overview](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/data_overview) confirms that TRAIN lacks `is_scored`, validation provides it, and the test callback never receives it. The platform applies the hidden test mask after inference. Every required row still needs a prediction, and every row updates state. Scoring intersects the mask with `need_prediction`; warm-up rows are never selected. Targets and predictions are clipped to ±2, target magnitude supplies the row weight, and eligible sequences receive equal final weight. No random-sampling rule, event threshold, target condition, or inclusion probability is stated there. The [FAQ](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/faq) restates masked scoring without explaining selection. The [rules](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/rules) distinguish public and private test populations but provide no mask mechanism. This establishes an unresolved documentation gap, not proof that no organizer explanation exists elsewhere.

## What the existing results actually compare

The selected model's initial TRAIN correlation was **0.358472415** on 1,020 eligible sequences from the fixed 1,024-group set. This is a target-clipped, **prediction-unclipped**, all-post-warm-up statistic. Its **0.638916085** screen WP uses different sequences, the supplied mask, and clipped predictions. The difference mixes selection, sequence population, clipping and eligibility; it is not a controlled estimate of a mask effect. [Training report](../artifacts/round2/affine_rho/report.json), [method](ROUND2_HEAD_METHOD.md), [screen results](ROUND2_RESULTS.md), [metric](../METRIC.md).

The weakly anchored hidden readout improved its training surrogate from **0.358472415 to 0.373269937**, while screen WP fell by **0.002446644**. The subsequent upper-GRU weighted-MSE trials lost **0.000104145** and **0.000317754** on the same screen, with both paired intervals below zero. These observations support caution about objective alignment and generalization. They do not isolate mask selection as the cause; overfitting, clipping changes and update direction remain alternatives. [Hidden fit](../artifacts/round2/head_rho_p1e-4/report.json), [head outcomes](ROUND2_RESULTS.md), [recurrent evidence](RECURRENT_PROGRESS.md).

Let `S` mean that a row is scored and `H` its causal feature history. TRAIN observes histories and labels, but never `S`. Many different functions `P(S=1 | H,y)` are compatible with exactly the same TRAIN distribution. Therefore no TRAIN-only classifier can recover this probability without additional assumptions or selection labels. Teacher confidence, target magnitude and event activity are possible training weights; none is an evidenced reconstruction of the official mask. This is an identifiability argument, not an organizer statement.

## Ranked actions and hypotheses

1. **First remove the known objective mismatch.** The already declared direct-clipped, equal-sequence Pearson trials address prediction clipping, centering and variance normalization that weighted MSE does not reproduce. Preserve their declared settings and candidate budget. Report their TRAIN statistic explicitly as all-row correlation. The prior hidden-head result shows why improvement still requires the frozen masked screen. This is the best-supported next action and adds no inference operations. A later TRAIN-only sequence holdout could distinguish fitting improvement from generalization before spending another screen; partition whole sequences, not adjacent rows.

2. **Prefer objective-preserving sampling before inventing a mask.** The metric already supplies `w=abs(clip(y,-2,2))`, normalized separately for each sequence and target. Extra magnitude weighting can accidentally square this emphasis. If a future implementation samples rows with known probability `q`, use `w/q` when estimating the weighted moment sums, preserving equal-sequence aggregation. Correlation is a ratio, so finite-sample estimates are not thereby unbiased. This can improve coverage or computational efficiency; it does not approximate unknown test inclusion probabilities. Targets used only to construct training loss or sampling weights introduce no inference dependency.

3. **If another arm becomes justified, test one bounded causal-activity mixture.** A concrete hypothesis is that a predictor benefits from emphasizing changing market states, irrespective of the unknown mask. Fit per-column scales on TRAIN only and define `d_t = mean_j min(abs(x_tj-x_(t-1)j)/s_j, c)`, then a bounded activity factor `g_t` normalized within each training sequence. Compare an explicitly declared small mixture `w'_t = w_t[(1-lambda)+lambda*g_t]` with identity weighting. Reset the previous row at each sequence; retain all rows and the original inference graph. This costs training arithmetic only. It needs no ordering assumption for anonymous book components. There is **no evidence** that the official mask favors active rows, so hard event filtering would be unjustified.

4. **Use TRAIN-only regime coverage as robustness, not mask prediction.** A future fixed holdout can separate label sign/magnitude and causal activity regimes and check whether updates hurt particular sequences. Cross-fit any learned reliability weight across whole TRAIN sequences; cap it and retain a positive weight floor. Do not downweight large teacher errors merely because they are difficult: that may suppress exactly the corrections sought. Any subsequent regime-weighted arm must be declared before validation outcomes. Anonymous targets do not justify guessed return horizons or financial event formulas.

The current protocol keeps validation labels and masks out of fitting and permanently excludes consumed confirmation. None of these hypotheses changes that boundary or guarantees improvement toward 0.75. Useful missing organizer information would be whether selection depends on observable causal inputs, future outcomes, or independent randomness, and whether the same procedure applies across validation/public/private sets.
